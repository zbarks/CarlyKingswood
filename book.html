Parenting a distressed child asks for something extraordinary.

Children who have been hurt often show their distress through anger, and sometimes through violence. For the adults who love them, this can be frightening, exhausting and isolating.

This book explains the biological, psychological and environmental reasons a child may behave in this way. It then offers practical ways to lower the chance of violence, calm a situation when it happens, and repair the relationship afterwards.

It also helps parents build a shared language with their child for emotions, and it speaks honestly about caring for yourself while you care for others.

The book does not claim to be a DDP manual, but it weaves in what parenting with an attitude of PACE looks like in everyday life. The DDP Network has published a review of the book that describes it as friendly, practical and non judgemental.
